## In one line
An App Shortcut exposes one or more of your app's **key actions or content to the whole system** (Siri, Spotlight, the Shortcuts app, the Action button, Apple Pencil squeeze) and is **usable the moment the app is installed**. Offer the **most common, important, self-contained tasks** (max **10**), give each **at most one familiar optional parameter**, keep the **spoken phrase short**, **ask a follow-up instead of demanding details up front**, **answer with enough text for audio-only devices**, make the shortcuts **discoverable inside the app**, and write the terms **"App Shortcuts" / "Shortcuts app" in title case with plural *Shortcuts***. Common domains should adopt **app schemas** first. Native platform feature; on the web the nearest things are manifest shortcuts, deep links and a command palette.

## Rules

### Framing (intro)
- App Shortcuts are started with **Siri, Spotlight, the Shortcuts app**, hardware such as the **Action button** (iPhone, Apple Watch) or a **squeeze of Apple Pencil**.
- They are **part of the app**, so they are **available right after installation**, even before the first launch (example: a journaling app's "new entry" shortcut). Once people use the app, the shortcuts can **reflect their choices** (example: calling recent contacts in FaceTime).
- They are built on **App Intents**: each App Shortcut contains **one or more actions**, i.e. a set of steps someone might want to run to finish a task (example: a home-security app combines "turn off the lights" + "lock the exterior doors" into one go-to-sleep shortcut).
- **Up to 10 App Shortcuts per app.**
- **Note (callout):** because the actions come from App Intents, people can also **build their own custom shortcuts in the Shortcuts app**, mixing actions from several apps and configuring their behaviour; Apple points to the Shortcuts User Guide for that.

### Best practices
- **should** **Consider app schemas first for common types of functionality.** Apps in common domain areas can adopt app schemas so their actions and content are available to **Apple Intelligence**; on supported devices Siri and other system experiences then surface app features **contextually, without adopting individual App Shortcuts**. (Added June 8, 2026.)
- **may** **Use App Shortcuts for what schemas don't cover**: unique features or custom content in areas outside the app schemas.
- **should** **Offer App Shortcuts for the app's most common and important tasks.** Simple tasks people can finish **without leaving their current context** work best; you may **open the app** when that makes a **multistep** task easier.
- **should** **Add flexibility with a set of options.** A shortcut may carry **a single optional value (parameter)** where it makes sense (example: "Start [morning, daily, sleep] meditation"). Include **predictable, familiar values** as options, because **people won't have the list in front of them**.
- **should** **Ask for clarification when optional information is missing.** Example: "Start meditation" without a type → follow up by suggesting the type used **most recently** or one that fits the **time of day**. If one option is most likely, **present it as the default** and give a **short list of alternatives**.
- **should** **Keep voice interactions simple.** If a phrase feels **too complicated to say aloud**, it's too hard to remember or say correctly. Example: "Start [sleep] meditation with nature sounds" looks like it has **two parameters** (type + sound); if extra information is truly required, **ask for it in a later step**. (Dialogue-writing guidance lives in the Siri page.)
- **should** **Make App Shortcuts discoverable in the app.** People remember shortcuts for tasks they do often, **once they know one exists**; show **occasional tips** when they perform a common action (developer API: `SiriTipUIView`).

#### Responding to App Shortcuts
- The app can respond with **spoken dialogue** and **custom visuals**:
  - **Snippets**: custom views for **static information or dialog options**, e.g. the weather at the person's location or an order confirmation.
  - **Live Activities**: **continuous access** to information that stays relevant and **changes over time**; good for **timers and countdowns** that stay until an event ends (developer API: `LiveActivityIntent`).
- **should** **Provide enough detail for audio-only devices** (AirPods, HomePod): people may **not be able to see the screen**, so put **all critical information in the full dialogue text** (developer API: `IntentDialog init(full:supporting:systemImageName:)`).

### Editorial guidelines
- **should** **Provide brief, memorable activation phrases and natural variants.** The phrase (or a variant you define) is what people **say to run the shortcut with Siri**, so keep it short. It **must include the app name**, but you can be creative around it. Example: Keynote accepts both "Create a Keynote" and "Add a new presentation in Keynote" for making a new document (developer API: `AppShortcutPhrase`).
- **must** **Write "App Shortcuts" and "Shortcuts app" in title case and keep *Shortcuts* plural.** Example sentence: "MyApp integrates with Shortcuts to provide a quick way to get things done with just a tap or by asking Siri, and offers App Shortcuts you can place on the Action button."
- **must** **Write individual shortcuts (not App Shortcuts, not the Shortcuts app) in lowercase.** Example: "Run a shortcut by asking Siri or tapping a suggestion on the Lock Screen."

### Platform considerations
- **iOS, iPadOS:** App Shortcuts can appear in the **Top Hit** area of **Spotlight** when people search for the app, or in the **Shortcuts area** below it. Each one has **an SF Symbol you choose** to represent it, **or a preview image** of an item the shortcut opens directly.
  - **should** **Order shortcuts by importance.** Your order sets how they **first** appear in **both Spotlight and the Shortcuts app**, so put the most generally useful first. After people start using them, the **system re-orders by frequency of use**.
- **macOS:** App Shortcuts are **not supported**, but **actions you build with App Intents are**, and people can build **custom shortcuts** from them in the Shortcuts app on Mac.
- **visionOS, watchOS:** no additional considerations. **tvOS:** not supported.

## Web translation
App Shortcuts are an **operating-system integration**: a web app cannot register with Siri, Spotlight or the Action button, and **must not fake those surfaces**. What transfers is the **design thinking**: a small, ranked set of self-contained, memorable actions, reachable from outside the app's main UI, each with at most one obvious parameter.

| HIG rule | Web implementation |
|---|---|
| Expose the app's key actions to the system, available from install | Installed PWAs: **Web App Manifest `shortcuts`** (long-press or right-click of the app icon; details, sizes and iOS gap in `home-screen-quick-actions.md`). Beyond the OS: **stable deep links** for every key action (`/notes/new`, `/compose?to=…`) so bookmarks, home-screen links, voice assistants, Shortcuts/automation tools and emails can reach it; a link works on the first visit, before any setup. |
| Max 10 shortcuts; only the most common, important tasks | Keep any shortcut list **short and ranked** (manifest: **1–4**, `home-screen-quick-actions.md`; the Apple cap of 10 is a ceiling, CONV). Choose tasks that **finish without leaving the current context** (a quick compose sheet, a toggle) and open the full app only for multistep tasks. |
| One optional parameter with familiar values | A **command with one argument**: `Start [morning / daily / sleep] meditation`. In a command palette (`role="combobox"` + `listbox`, `aria-activedescendant`) show the verb phrase first and the argument as a **chip or second step** with the familiar values listed; in a URL: `?type=sleep`, and a **sensible default** when it's missing. |
| Ask a follow-up when the optional value is missing; default = last used / time-of-day guess, short list of alternatives | If the link or command arrives **without the parameter**, **don't error**: open the small chooser with the **most likely value preselected** (last used, or by local time) and **2–4 alternatives** (CONV) beneath; one Enter/tap confirms. |
| Keep voice phrases simple, at most one parameter | For **voice-enabled** web UI (Web Speech API, assistant integration), keep commands to **verb + object (+ one value)**; ask for anything else in a **later step**. Test by saying the phrase aloud. Same rule for **command-palette entries**: short verb-first titles ("New note"), synonyms searchable but not displayed. |
| Phrase must include the app name; brief, memorable variants | For a voice/assistant surface include the **product name** once ("Add a note in Nonplo"); define **2–3 natural variants** in the command's `keywords`/aliases so free typing ("new note", "create note", "add note") finds it. In visible UI the manifest/menu label **omits the app name** (the OS already shows it; see `home-screen-quick-actions.md`). |
| Discoverable in the app: occasional tips | **Contextual, dismissible tips** after someone does a common action manually ("You can also press ⌘K, then type *new note*", "Add this to your home screen for one-tap access"): **shown at most a few times**, never blocking, never repeated after dismissal (`offering-help.md`, `onboarding.md`); a **Shortcuts/Commands** entry in Help lists them all. |
| Respond with snippets (static/interactive views) | The web analogue of a **snippet** is a **small inline result** without leaving context: a confirmation card, toast, popover or compact sheet with the outcome and **one or two actions** ("Order placed · Undo · View"). Announce it (`role="status"`, `aria-live="polite"`; Feedback gate) instead of `alert()`. |
| Respond with Live Activities for timers/countdowns | A **persistent, glanceable status** for a task with a clear end: a sticky chip/banner or the **Media Session / Badging API / `document.title`** update; compute remaining time from a **timestamp**, not by ticking a counter (`workouts.md`, `live-viewing-apps.md`). It ends when the event ends. |
| Enough detail for audio-only responses | Every result must **stand alone as text**: a full sentence with the critical facts ("Your 2:30 PM haircut is booked for Friday, 3 October"), in a live region so **screen-reader** and **voice** users get the same information as sighted ones; **never** convey the outcome only through an icon, colour or animation. |
| Title case for "App Shortcuts"/"Shortcuts app", plural; lowercase for individual shortcuts | Applies **when the copy names Apple's features** (e.g. a help article on setting up a Siri/Shortcuts integration): "App Shortcuts", "the Shortcuts app", "a shortcut" (lowercase). Don't write "Siri shortcut(s) app" or singular "Shortcut app". Keep the term list (`writing.md`) in one glossary. For our own commands use the capitalisation table in `writing.md` (menu items, button labels: Title Case). |
| Order by importance first; the system then follows usage | Ship a **hand-ranked default order**; if you personalise, **promote by frequency of use** but keep the top item stable enough to be predictable, and never reorder while a menu is open (CONV). Recents and most-used are common in palettes ("Recent" group first). |
| SF Symbol or preview image per shortcut | **Lucide/Phosphor/Ionicons** glyph (never SF Symbols artwork) or a **thumbnail of the linked item** (note, document, contact) for content shortcuts; keep one style and weight across the list (`icons.md`). |
| App schemas / Apple Intelligence surface app features contextually | The web/AI-agent counterpart is **exposing well-typed actions** (an **MCP server's tools**, an OpenAPI action list) so assistants can call them without custom integration (CONV analogy, not something the page says). Follow the same discipline: **few, clear, named actions with typed parameters**, and confirm before anything with side effects. |
| macOS: not supported, but the underlying actions are | Don't assume every surface gets every entry point: **the action must exist as a normal in-app command too** and, if the OS surface is missing, **offer the same action elsewhere** (menu, palette, link). |
| Custom shortcuts built by the user from your actions | Where the product allows automation, let people **compose your actions** (Zapier/webhooks/MCP, rules builders) and give each action **stable names, inputs and outputs**. |

## Checklist
- [ ] The **key, self-contained tasks** (≤ 10, in practice 1–4 for a web manifest) are reachable from outside the main UI, ranked by importance, and every one has a **stable deep link** and an in-app equivalent.
- [ ] Each shortcut has **at most one optional parameter** with familiar values; a missing value triggers a **follow-up with a default and a short alternative list**, not an error.
- [ ] Phrases and labels are **short enough to say aloud**, contain the product name where a voice surface requires it, and have natural variants/aliases.
- [ ] Shortcuts are **discoverable in the app** through occasional, dismissible, contextual tips (and a full list in Help).
- [ ] Responses are **inline and short** (snippet-like) or a **persistent status** for timers, and **every result is complete as text** for audio/screen-reader users.
- [ ] Apple feature names in copy: **App Shortcuts / Shortcuts app** in Title Case with plural *Shortcuts*; individual shortcuts in lowercase.
- [ ] No fake Siri/Spotlight surface is drawn on the web; OS integration goes through the manifest or standard APIs.
- [ ] Icons are Lucide/Phosphor/Ionicons (or item thumbnails), never SF Symbols artwork.
